# “职”此无忧 · ZCWY

> **Let every part-time job come with a trustworthy record of rights and work.**

ZCWY is a platform designed to protect the rights of university students in part-time work while encouraging trust and responsible participation on both sides.

We explore how verifiable records, smart contracts, and blockchain can reshape trust in part-time employment.

Students should be accountable for the work they take on. Employers should be accountable for the promises they make. And every important step in between should leave a trace.

## What You Can Explore in the Demo

### Student Side

- Part-time work dashboard
- Job opportunities and application tracking
- Work progress
- Deliverable submission
- Payment status
- Verified experience certificates

### Employer Side

- Task management
- Student work records
- Deliverable review
- Payment settlement
- Employer verification status

### Rights Protection

- Payment escrow
- Records of key events
- A complete evidence trail
- Simulated smart contract execution
- Verifiable records of work experience

## Core Demo Flow

```text
Student submits deliverables
→ Employer reviews and approves them
→ Simulated smart contract settlement
→ Payment received
→ Experience certificate generated
```

**A part-time job should leave behind more than a paycheck.**

It can also become a trustworthy, verifiable record of real-world experience.

## Project Status

ZCWY is currently an interactive product concept prototype.

The business data, blockchain records, and smart contract execution shown in the project all run in a simulated environment. The current prototype is not connected to real blockchain nodes, payment systems, identity verification services, or a production backend.

This demo is not trying to answer the question:

> **“Can we put blockchain into a part-time job platform?”**

We care more about a different question:

> **Can technology make relationships that once relied on verbal promises clearer, more trustworthy, and easier to prove?**

## The Story Behind Open-Sourcing

This project began as an entry for a university student competition and as our attempt to explore a product solution for protecting students in part-time work.

But we felt that ideas about fairness, trust, and young people's rights did not have to remain inside a competition submission or a private repository.

So we decided to open it up.

This is also the first project I have officially open-sourced. I am glad that my first one happens to begin with a problem as real and concrete as student part-time work.

Whether you are looking for inspiration, want to reuse parts of the interface, interactions, or project structure, or simply want to clone the repository and see how it works, feel free to explore.

If you find a problem, have an idea, or see something that could be improved, you are also welcome to open an Issue. I will do my best to respond.
